        How it works

        Discover

        Every publicly trusted certificate is logged in Certificate Transparency. Cert Radar asks Cert Spotter for the certificates valid today, then crt.sh for the full history when it is available. Renewals of the same set of names are grouped into one lineage.

        Classify

        A certificate looks manual when it comes from a CA with no sign of ACME use, runs close to the maximum lifetime, renews on an irregular rhythm, or renews at the last minute. Steady renewals well before expiry look automated. Each row shows its reasons.

        Forecast

        Each manual certificate is projected under the 200, 100 and 47-day caps, renewing about a week before expiry. Unclear ones count as half. The grade weighs the manual share, lapses between expiry and renewal, last-minute habits and anything that needs attention now.

        Passed by the CA/Browser Forum in April 2025 with Apple, Google, Mozilla and Microsoft voting in favour. From 2029, every renewal more than 10 days after the last validation needs a fresh DNS or HTTP check.

        Watch your domains for free

        Fork the repository, list your domains in watchlist.txt, and GitHub Actions scans them every day, runs live TLS checks and opens an issue when something is about to expire. GitHub emails you about the issue.

        Use it in CI

        node scripts/scan.js example.com --live --fail-under C prints the report and fails the build when readiness drops below a grade. No dependencies.

        What it can't see

        Certificate Transparency shows what was issued, not what is deployed, and private or internal certificates never appear in it. A commercial CA's ACME service can't be told apart from a manual order, which is why classification is a guess with reasons.
